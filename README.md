# Code-A-Discrete-Time-Model-of-Delayed-Allergic-Skin-Reactions-to-Intradermal-Tattoos

A Discrete Time Model of Delayed Allergic Skin Reactions to Intradermal Tattoos

Allergic skin reactions from a tattoo occur approximately 6-10% of the time. These reactions can be delayed weeks, months, or even years post-application. Problematically, these reactions can be difficult to anticipate. Patch test methods insufficiently determine risk due to topical application and discounting the accumulation of secondary irritants. Thus, a model is beneficial in order to analyze these dynamics and predict long-term outcomes. Our model is a system of four coupled difference equations with a compartmental structure, inspired by the basic SIR model, representing the physical state of the skin. We consider influential factors such as personal sensitivity, UV exposure, and long-term allergen accumulation. Mathematical analysis identified the endemic equilibrium, sensitivity-free equilibrium, and their respective stabilities, as well as the explicit solution to the equation representing allergen accumulation. Numerical simulations explore long-term sensitivity and recovery trajectories. Because clinical patient data is sparse, we created several noisy synthetic datasets and trained Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to successfully retrieve the parameters. Finally, our sensitivity analysis indicated the relative strength of each parameter over a period of 150 weeks. Overall, this work contributes to the current understanding of tattoo skin reactions by offering a compartmental model capturing the complex post-application skin environment over the short and long term.

This repository contains codes used for numerical simulations (Euler's method), Sobol sensitivity analysis, and PINNs.
